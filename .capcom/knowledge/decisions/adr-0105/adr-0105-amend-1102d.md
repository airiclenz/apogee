---
title: "ADR 0105 amendment of 2026-11-02 (d): the engine owns a seat's fallover follow-through"
---

Decided with the seat and resolved-entry plan (docs/plans/2026-11-02 - 04 - seat-and-resolved-entry-plan.md, items 5, 6, 7 and 9). It records that the engine, not the Driver, owns what follows a landed fallover: the seat's one announcement and the Sub-agents seat's re-latch. It amends decision 6 (every agent on the seat moves with it, and the seat emits one event) and decision 8 (the resolver owns the single-flight and the fallen-over state). It supersedes the Server fallback follow-ups' (docs/plans/archived/2026-11-02 - 01 - fallback-followups-plan.md) "Driver-side" Firing re-latch call and its claim-once `Announce` on the answer.

**The engine announces a fallover once, whatever resolver it was handed.** The resolver still owns the grace, the walk and the single-flight (decision 8); it no longer owns the announcement, and `FallbackAnswer` carries no `Announce` claim. On the session seat the Agents share one Upstream holder, so the one Agent whose replace moves the holder announces. On the Sub-agents seat each routed child speaks over its own holder, so the announcement is a claim-once in the delegation latch the whole tree shares, keyed by the seat's resolver and the server it moved to; it is claimed only after the claimant's replace moved, so a caller cancelled before it moved anything never spends it. Where the resolver's dynamic type is not comparable, the claim keys on the seat's holder instead. A bench, or any Driver that supplies its own resolver, gets one `upstream_switched` Event per move with no bookkeeping of its own (ADR 0031).

**The engine re-latches a fallen-over Sub-agents seat.** The Driver resolves the fallback's Delegation target whole from the beat that found the fallback answering, and hands it to the engine on the answer (`FallbackAnswer.Target`, carrying the seat's own resolver as its Fallback); a session-seat answer may state the fallback's fan-out width (`FallbackAnswer.ParallelAgents`, 0 leaves it alone). When the engine first observes a routed landing in the tree — a routed child's consult answered with the switch, or, where every caller was cancelled during the grace, the engine's own consult joining the run that went on — it re-latches the tree's Delegation target to that target and states its width as the far width on the root. Each (resolver, server) landing re-latches once, so a straggler moves nothing, and only while the latch is still armed on that resolver: `SetDelegationSeat` (a `/sub-agents-server` switch, or a relist that moves the key) disarms it, so the human's choice wins. A seat whose resolver's dynamic type is not comparable (an embedder's func adapter) is never re-latched, since the engine cannot tell that resolver is still the armed one. A nil target re-latches nothing. The Drivers no longer push the target: the latch slot the binary held for it and `run.Spec`'s `BindDelegationLatch` are deleted, and the TUI's landing hook keeps only the re-point of its beat onto the fallback entry. Every Driver — the TUI, headless, the daemon, a Firing — gets the same re-latch, at the timing `adr-0045-amend-1102` asked for: a routed child spawned before the next beat dials the fallback.
