---
title: "ADR 0019 amendment of 2026-11-11: a served PDF carries a policy without the bare sandbox"
---

**Why now.** The code audit of 2026-11-10 (`docs/reviews/code-audit-2026-11-10.md`, issue
`served-pdf-csp-sandbox`) found that the fourth amendment's (b) put **every** document the doc
server answers under one policy, `.pdf` included, although that policy exists for the active
formats alone. Ratified by the owner on 2026-11-11 (`docs/plans/2026-11-11 - 01 -
fence-escape-and-open-defects-plan.md`, design call "PDF policy").

**(a) A served PDF carries its own policy, without the bare sandbox.** Rung 2 answers
`application/pdf` with `Content-Security-Policy: frame-ancestors 'none'; form-action 'none';
base-uri 'none'` — no `sandbox`, no `default-src`, so no implied `object-src 'none'` — still beside
`X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff`. `.html`, `.htm`, `.svg` and every other type keep the fourth
amendment's bare-sandbox policy unchanged. This supersedes the fourth amendment's (b) sentence that
every document the doc server answers carries the bare-sandbox policy: every document but a PDF
does.

**(b) Why the sandbox goes for a PDF.** Chromium has a history of rendering a PDF blank when its
response carries a CSP `sandbox` (crbug 413851 "Sandbox breaks PDF rendering"; crbug 1187122,
reported fixed in M91 and seen again on Chrome 108), and `default-src 'none'` implies the
`object-src 'none'` a built-in viewer can trip over. `.pdf` is the one format both rungs show, and
on an SSH devbox rung 2 is the only rung that reaches the user, so a blank page there would be
invisible to apogee. Dropping the two directives costs nothing the fourth amendment bought: a PDF
cannot carry the `<meta http-equiv="refresh">` the bare `sandbox` exists to stop, and `nosniff`
pins the type, so the bytes cannot be re-read as markup. The three directives that remain have no
`default-src` fallback and refuse framing, form posts and base rewriting on their own.

**(c) The policy is chosen from the media type, and the closed one is the default.** The server
names `application/pdf` as the single exception and falls every other type — including any format
that becomes active later — to the bare-sandbox policy, so the fourth amendment's "a format that
becomes active later is already covered" still holds. A server test pins the exact PDF policy and,
separately, the bare sandbox on `.html`, `.htm` and `.svg`.

**Not yet confirmed in a browser.** The change rests on the Chromium record, not on a rendering
observed here; opening a served PDF in current Chrome, Firefox and Safari stays open as a manual
check on `served-pdf-csp-sandbox`.
